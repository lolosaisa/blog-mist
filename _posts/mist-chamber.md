The blockchain industry has always struggled with a fundamental tension: **how do we achieve privacy without compromising compliance?**  
Most privacy tools hide *too much*, making them incompatible with KYC/AML requirements. Meanwhile, fully transparent chains expose sensitive financial data.

**MIST Chamber** solves this problem by introducing a *compliance-aligned privacy model* powered by **zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs)** and the **FOCBB Protocol**—a breakthrough that allows users to make private transfers while still proving regulatory requirements.

---

## 🔐 Why MIST Chamber Exists

Traditional privacy tools (mixers, stealth wallets) break compliance entirely. Regulators classify them as “obscuring technologies,” and users risk becoming unbanked.

But enterprises, institutions, and individuals all need the *same things*:

- Privacy from the public  
- Transparency toward authorized auditors  
- Compliance with financial regulations  
- A user experience that feels like normal crypto

**MIST Chamber delivers this balance**, enabling:

✔ Private transfers  
✔ Selective disclosure for auditors  
✔ Proof-of-compliance without revealing transaction data  
✔ Complete user anonymity at the public-chain level  

---

## 🧠 How It Works (Simplified)

MIST Chamber uses a layered system:

1. **Deposit Layer**  
   Assets enter a private vault where balances are represented as encrypted commitments.

2. **ZKP Transaction Layer**  
   Users transfer funds privately by generating a proof that:
   - They own the funds  
   - The transaction is valid  
   - No rules are broken  
   without revealing amounts or recipient.

3. **Compliance Registry**  
   Instead of exposing transactions, users submit *proofs* that they meet regulatory checks.

This preserves privacy *and* allows institutions to satisfy financial laws.

---

## 🧾 Compliance Without Surveillance

The system supports:

- **Proof-of-Source-of-Funds**  
- **Proof-of-Identity (Selective Disclosure)**  
- **Proof-of-Non-Sanctioned Activity**  
- **Proof-of-Limits (e.g., AML thresholds)**

All without revealing:

- Account balances  
- Transaction history  
- Recipient identity  
- Amounts sent
